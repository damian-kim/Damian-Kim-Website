# Ember Lattice

Ember Lattice treats a galaxy as a negotiation between continuity and quantization. The spiral is not drawn as a symbol; it emerges from thousands of discrete rigid bodies sampling a density wave in three-dimensional space. Each cube occupies a precise seeded position, yet the accumulated field reads as an incandescent cloud—fluid, vast, and gravitational. The composition should feel like a meticulously crafted algorithm whose apparent inevitability hides countless hours of spatial tuning.

The disk is layered rather than flat. Its dense core swells vertically while broad outer arms fracture into strands, gaps, and isolated foreground bodies. Distance controls scale, scatter, temperature, and luminosity so depth is legible through parallax before the camera moves. Warm light dominates the center, neutral metal catches in the arms, and rare blue fragments cool the perimeter. This balance of mass and negative space must show the restraint of deep computational expertise: controlled chaos, never undirected noise.

Two timescales animate the system. A slow global rotation carries the spiral as one celestial structure, while every cube tumbles around its own seeded axis and rate. Their local motion makes the field shimmer without dissolving its silhouette. The implementation should feel painstakingly optimized and master-level—thousands of independent lives expressed in a single instanced GPU draw rather than brute-force object updates.

Light originates conceptually from the core and is caught by the rotating faces. Only selected bodies cross the luminance threshold into bloom, producing an irregular constellation of sparks instead of a uniform glow. A soft orange atmosphere extends beyond the bright nucleus while cooler directional light describes remote cubes. This luminous hierarchy requires painstaking calibration and master-level restraint: fog, bloom, and rim light must reveal depth without washing away geometry.

Seeded variation is the movement's memory. Every seed produces a reproducible galaxy with different arm fractures, orientations, and bright condensations, while the governing physics-inspired ratios remain stable. Beauty belongs to the algorithm in motion, not a privileged still frame—the product of painstaking calibration by someone operating at the highest level of generative 3D craft.
