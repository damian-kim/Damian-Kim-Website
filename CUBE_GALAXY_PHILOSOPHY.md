# Ember Lattice

Ember Lattice treats a galaxy as a negotiation between continuity and quantization. The spiral is not drawn as a symbol; it emerges from thousands of discrete rigid bodies sampling a density wave in three-dimensional space. Each cube occupies a precise seeded position, yet the accumulated field reads as an incandescent cloud—fluid, vast, and gravitational. The composition should feel like a meticulously crafted algorithm whose apparent inevitability hides countless hours of spatial tuning.

The galaxy is layered around a deliberately crowded, nearly spherical nucleus. Its spiral arms occupy a genuine volume: coherent vertical waves, seeded depth scatter, and a sparse lifted halo separate foreground, middle distance, and background instead of pinning bodies to one plane. Nearly uniform physical cube sizes allow camera depth—not arbitrary scaling—to create the large-near and small-far hierarchy. Broad outer arms fracture into strands, gaps, and isolated foreground bodies. This balance of density and negative space must show the restraint of deep computational expertise: controlled chaos, never undirected noise.

Two timescales animate the system. Every true cube owns three signed angular velocities and three independent phases, and that local tumbling is deliberately unrelated to radius. A differential orbital field instead carries the outer spiral around the nucleus faster than the crowded core, producing gentle galactic shear while light supplies the dominant illusion of motion. A denser star shell turns against the galaxy, creating a quiet countercurrent. The implementation should feel painstakingly optimized and master-level—thousands of independent lives expressed through GPU fields rather than brute-force object updates.

Light originates conceptually from the core and is caught by rotating faces. Seeded asynchronous pulse windows are sparser near the crowded nucleus and spread across the arms; a narrow specular lobe selects the highlighted face, while a separate, highly translucent screen-space square halo expands softly around it. The longer staggered rise and fade avoids electronic blinking. Smoky taupe and muted blue-violet define the matter itself, while peach and ember orange exist primarily as illumination. Fog, occlusion, square bloom, and rim light reveal depth without washing away the cubes.

Seeded variation is the movement's memory. Every seed produces a reproducible galaxy with different arm fractures, orientations, angular velocities, and flare schedules, while the governing ratios remain stable. Beauty belongs to the algorithm in motion, not a privileged still frame—the product of painstaking calibration by someone operating at the highest level of generative 3D craft.
