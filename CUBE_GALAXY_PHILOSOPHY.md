# Ember Lattice

Ember Lattice treats a galaxy as a negotiation between continuity and quantization. The spiral is not drawn as a symbol; it emerges from thousands of discrete rigid bodies sampling a density wave in three-dimensional space. Each cube occupies a precise seeded position, yet the accumulated field reads as an incandescent cloud—fluid, vast, and gravitational. The composition should feel like a meticulously crafted algorithm whose apparent inevitability hides countless hours of spatial tuning.

The disk is layered around a deliberately crowded nucleus. Nearly uniform physical cube sizes allow camera depth—not arbitrary scaling—to create the large-near and small-far hierarchy. Broad outer arms fracture into strands, gaps, and isolated foreground bodies while warm light dominates the center and rare blue fragments cool the perimeter. This balance of density and negative space must show the restraint of deep computational expertise: controlled chaos, never undirected noise.

Two timescales animate the system. A slow global rotation carries the spiral as one structure, while every true cube owns three signed angular velocities and three independent phases. Outer bodies tumble somewhat faster as a perspective cue, but the gradient remains subtle enough that light supplies the dominant illusion of motion. A denser star shell turns against the galaxy, creating a quiet countercurrent. The implementation should feel painstakingly optimized and master-level—thousands of independent lives expressed through GPU fields rather than brute-force object updates.

Light originates conceptually from the core and is caught by rotating faces. Seeded asynchronous pulse windows decide which bodies awaken, a narrow specular lobe selects the highlighted face, and a separate screen-space square halo expands softly around it. The result is a staggered rise, brief white flare, and faded geometric afterimage rather than electronic blinking. This luminous hierarchy requires painstaking calibration and master-level restraint: fog, square bloom, and rim light must reveal depth without washing away the cubes.

Seeded variation is the movement's memory. Every seed produces a reproducible galaxy with different arm fractures, orientations, angular velocities, and flare schedules, while the governing ratios remain stable. Beauty belongs to the algorithm in motion, not a privileged still frame—the product of painstaking calibration by someone operating at the highest level of generative 3D craft.
